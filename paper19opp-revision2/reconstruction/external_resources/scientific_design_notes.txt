Independent-unit and hypothesis audit

The proposed question is scientifically useful but must be narrowed to what the observational design identifies. Replogle, Norman and Frangieh profile pooled perturbation endpoints. They do not measure the pretreatment expression state of each subsequently perturbed cell. A control cell cannot be paired retrospectively to an individual treated cell. Clustering treated endpoint expression and calling that the baseline state would condition on the response and create outcome leakage.

A defensible primary question is whether matched control-reference context features improve held-out response prediction or predict response reproducibility beyond perturbation identity, response magnitude, library complexity and cell support. This is context-level prediction, not proof that baseline stress mechanistically causes a response. Validation must use common measured genes and frozen modules, fit every transformation on discovery data, and compare against context-free and simple response-magnitude baselines at identical task coverage.

The effective number of baseline-state observations is the number of independently measured contexts or studies, not the number of genes sharing a control reference. Repeated genes, reused controls and technical capture lanes induce dependence. Three to five studies are enough to characterize transfer in these particular studies but cannot support precise universal effects of baseline stress independent of assay, modality, dose, timing and laboratory. In particular, CRISPRa versus CRISPRi versus knockout can reverse the expected sign of a gene response; a common latent label does not remove this intervention difference.

MIX-Seq has many characterized cell-line contexts and is best suited to estimating context-level predictive relationships, provided matched-horizon DMSO inputs are explicitly identified as experimentally available control measurements. A model using them is not a pretreatment-only intervention policy. Inferred target-program probabilities remain transcriptomic tail fractions, not death or proliferation probabilities.

The independent candidate test is stronger when discovery ranking, primary program, sign, candidate eligibility, Top10/Top20 cutoffs, normalization and null comparisons are frozen before independent expression outcomes are evaluated. An independent K562 CRISPRi study tests same-context replication; an independent melanoma CRISPR knockout study tests more demanding contextual transfer but also introduces modality and biological differences. A lack of transfer should revise the biological claim, not trigger outcome-based reranking. Validation should compare both directional target effects and nuisance-program effects; success of a ranking formula does not validate a control node or a mechanistic regulator.

Data-coverage gates may use metadata before unblinding, but they change the target population. In Frangieh, low surviving-cell numbers for many overlapping essential genes imply survivor selection, not merely a random reduction in precision. Additional gene-level target-expression adjustment helps characterize observability and perturbation efficacy; it cannot recover missing killed cells. Control-standardized program shifts may be interpretable even across contexts, while their interpretation as functional cell death, division or fate requires independent phenotypic assays.

For baseline-state biological validation, the cleanest feasible follow-up among existing resources is an independently generated lineage or serial-treatment experiment in which a clone is sampled before treatment and its relatives are assayed later. Descendant support and clone abundance must be handled explicitly. The locally available Schaff serial-treatment study and publicly verified CellTag reprogramming study are usable resources for a separate analysis, but neither was analyzed in this task and their assay-specific outcomes are not interchangeable with LARRY branch rank.
